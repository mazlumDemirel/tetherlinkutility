How it works
STEP 01

Tap START SERVER

The Wi-Fi hotspot turns on automatically, no need to dig through system settings first. The app creates a local SOCKS5 proxy tunnel and displays the connection details.

STEP 02

Show the QR code

One QR code carries the Wi-Fi network and the proxy address together. The network name and password are also shown as plain text for laptops.

STEP 03

Connect your device

Android: scan the QR code, on most devices, Wi-Fi joins and the proxy connects automatically. If it doesn't, join the Wi-Fi shown and scan again. Windows / macOS / iOS: join the Wi-Fi shown, then enter the IP and port in your SOCKS5 proxy settings.

STEP 04

Start browsing

Traffic routes through your mobile data. No remote server, everything stays on your device.

Using your phone's own hotspot? If you turn on Mobile Hotspot (Android) or Personal Hotspot (iPhone) yourself, save its name and password once under WI-FI in the app. They stay on your device, the hotspot itself isn't changed, and they're added to the QR code so other devices join by scanning. Not needed when the Android app starts its own hotspot.

Frequently asked questions

How does Tether Link Utility work?

It runs a local SOCKS5 proxy on your Android phone, using Android's VpnService API, and shares it over the phone's Wi-Fi hotspot (no USB cable involved). Your other devices send their traffic to that proxy, and the phone forwards it over its mobile data connection. There's no remote server in between.

Do I need root access?

No, it works entirely without root privileges.

Does it use a remote server?

No. Proxied traffic goes from your devices to your phone and out over your mobile data; it is not routed through, logged, or stored on any server of ours. The free version shows Google AdMob ads, and the app uses Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, which collect device identifiers and usage and crash data, as described in our privacy policy.

Can I connect a Windows or Mac laptop?

Yes, point your laptop's SOCKS5 proxy settings (built into macOS, or via Firefox/FoxyProxy on Windows) at the IP and port shown in the app. That covers apps which check that setting; for full system-wide coverage including DNS, use sing-box in TUN mode instead.

Does a SOCKS5 proxy setting protect all my traffic?

Not entirely. A browser or system SOCKS5 setting only reroutes traffic from apps that check it; DNS lookups and other background traffic can still go over the plain hotspot connection instead of the proxy. For full coverage on Windows or macOS, run sing-box in TUN mode, which routes your whole system's traffic, DNS included, through the tunnel.

Does it work with iPhone?

iOS has no native SOCKS5 setting, but apps like Shadowrocket or Potatso can connect to the proxy the same way, for that app's own traffic only, not the whole device. A verified full-device option isn't currently available on iOS. A native iOS app is in development and coming soon.

How many devices can connect at once?

The free tier connects up to 2 devices at once; Pro raises that to 5. Pro is $2.99/month or $12.99/year (USD), and prices may vary by country.

Does it work with 5G and 4G/LTE?

Yes, it shares whatever active mobile data connection your phone currently has, regardless of network generation.

Does it use my mobile data plan?

Yes. Data used by connected devices counts toward your plan. Check your carrier's terms for tethering.

Is it free?

Yes, the free version is free to use: up to 2 devices at once, with ads. Pro removes ads and allows up to 5 devices at once. The app itself doesn't currently add a speed limit; actual speed depends on your carrier, plan and signal.

Can other devices join my own hotspot by scanning the QR code?

Yes. If you turn on your phone's system hotspot yourself (Mobile Hotspot on Android, Personal Hotspot on iPhone), save its network name and password once under WI-FI in the app. They are stored only on your device, the app doesn't change your hotspot settings, and the details are added to the QR code so other devices join the network automatically by scanning. On iPhone, the password is shown in Settings > Personal Hotspot. When the Android app starts its own hotspot automatically, this step isn't needed.
