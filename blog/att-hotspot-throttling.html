AT&T Hotspot Throttling Explained

Published September 18, 2026

AT&T's unlimited plans separate "on-device" data from "hotspot" data the same way most major US carriers do, and hotspot data is the one that runs out first if you're using your phone as your main connection for a laptop.

How AT&T's hotspot allotment works

AT&T's unlimited tiers have historically included a monthly high-speed hotspot data allotment that varies significantly by plan level, lower tiers see a smaller allotment after which the carrier's network can deprioritize hotspot traffic during congestion, while the top-tier plan has historically carried little to no hotspot-specific deprioritization at all. Exact allotments and deprioritization rules change between plan revisions and differ by tier, check your current plan's terms on AT&T's own site for the number that applies to you.

How AT&T detects tethered traffic

As with other carriers, AT&T doesn't need you to declare tethering, it's inferred from traffic characteristics like TTL (Time To Live) decrement and deep packet inspection (DPI) patterns. Read the full mechanics of how this detection works.

Deprioritization is a softer form of throttling than a hard speed cap, it only bites during network congestion. On an uncongested tower at 2am, hotspot traffic that's technically "used up its allotment" may still run at full speed; the slowdown shows up specifically when the local network is busy.
