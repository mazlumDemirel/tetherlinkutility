AT&T Hotspot Speed & Data Limits Explained (2026)

Published September 18, 2026 · Updated September 25, 2026

AT&T's unlimited plans separate data used on the phone from hotspot data, the same way most major US carriers do. If you use your phone as the main connection for a laptop, the hotspot allowance is usually the one that runs out first.

What AT&T's hotspot limit is

AT&T's unlimited plans include a set amount of high-speed hotspot data per line each month, and the amount varies by plan. According to AT&T's "Use Your Device's Hotspot" support page, once you use all of it, your hotspot speed slows to a maximum of 128 Kbps unless you add more hotspot data. This is a fixed speed limit, so it applies whether or not the network is busy. (Checked September 2026 against AT&T's support page, last updated August 26, 2026, and its Unlimited plan terms. Allowances differ by plan, confirm yours in myAT&T.)

At 128 Kbps, messaging and basic email usually still work, while video calls, streaming and large downloads generally do not. If your laptop suddenly feels unusable late in the month, the hotspot allowance is the first thing to check.

How to check your remaining AT&T hotspot data

  1. myAT&T app or website: sign in, choose your line and open the usage section. Plans with a hotspot allowance show hotspot usage for the current billing cycle.
  2. Your phone's data counter: on Android, the hotspot or data usage screen shows how much connected devices used. On iPhone, Settings → Cellular lists Personal Hotspot usage.
  3. Billing cycle date: note when your cycle resets, since the allowance refreshes then.

For other carriers, see how to check if your hotspot is really unlimited.

Official options when it runs out

  1. Add hotspot data if AT&T offers it for your plan. Availability and pricing vary, so check myAT&T.
  2. Move to a plan with a larger hotspot allowance if you tether regularly.
  3. Wait for your billing cycle to reset, and use Wi-Fi for large downloads and updates in the meantime.
