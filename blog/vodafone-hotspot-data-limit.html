Vodafone Hotspot Data Limit Explained

Published September 21, 2026

Vodafone (Turkey) tracks hotspot/tethering data separately from your normal on-device browsing, using its own dedicated allowances rather than one shared pool. That split is exactly why sharing your connection can run out fast even when your regular plan still shows plenty of data left.

How Vodafone splits hotspot data

A typical Vodafone account shows this broken out clearly under "Kalan Kullanımlarım" (My Remaining Usage) in the app: a dedicated Hotspot Internet allowance bundled into the plan (commonly a fixed amount like 15GB, tracked and exhausted independently of your normal data), plus any Paylaşım (sharing) add-on packages purchased separately (for example a 50GB package sold specifically for tethering). Once the bundled hotspot allowance hits 0, you're drawing from the paid add-on package if you have one, not from your regular plan's remaining data.

We tested this ourselves. With a Vodafone sharing package sitting at 583.5MB remaining, we ran our Mac sing-box tunnel and downloaded a 1GB test file through it. Checked the package again afterward: still 583.5MB, unchanged, while our own app's counter showed the full transfer routed through the tunnel. See the full video proof.

How Vodafone detects tethered traffic

As with other carriers, this is inferred from the traffic itself, commonly via TTL (Time To Live) decrement on packets forwarded from a connected device, and traffic-pattern analysis. See our full explanation of how carrier hotspot detection works for the mechanics behind this.

Your options

  1. Check "Kalan Kullanımlarım" in the Vodafone app before you tether, so you know which bucket (bundled hotspot allowance vs. a paid sharing package) you're about to draw from.
  2. Buy a Paylaşım (sharing) package if you consistently need more hotspot data than the bundled allowance covers.
  3. Run a local proxy instead of a plain hotspot, apps like Tether Link Utility route that traffic through a local proxy instead of a plain hotspot connection, without root or a remote server, as shown in the test above.
