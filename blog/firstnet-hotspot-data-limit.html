Does FirstNet Throttle Hotspot Data?

Published September 23, 2026

FirstNet is AT&T's dedicated network for verified first responders and public safety agencies, and its hotspot rules work differently from a regular AT&T consumer plan. If you're used to consumer "unlimited" plans quietly capping hotspot data in a separate bucket, FirstNet's model is genuinely different, though not restriction-free.

What FirstNet Responder plans actually include

FirstNet's Responder Unlimited plans are marketed with unlimited data, mobile hotspot, and tethering included for the primary device, with tethering available to up to five simultaneous devices on eligible plans. That's a meaningfully different starting point from consumer AT&T, Verizon, or T-Mobile unlimited tiers, which almost always carve hotspot data into its own smaller, separately-metered allotment.

The real limits that do apply

  • A usage-pattern threshold, not a per-month hard cap: according to FirstNet's own published plan documentation, a line that exceeds roughly 22GB of data in a month for three consecutive months can be required to move from an unlimited plan to a pooled data plan. This isn't the same as hotspot-specific throttling mid-cycle, it's a longer-term usage pattern check.
  • No unattended, continuous hotspot use as home or office broadband: FirstNet's terms restrict using a phone, hotspot, or router as a substitute for fixed residential or commercial internet service, this is aimed at continuous, unattended connections rather than normal on-shift tethering.
  • Device and plan eligibility: the tethering device needs to be FirstNet-capable and provisioned correctly, and exact terms differ across Primary, Extended Primary, and Agency-paid plan variants.

Plan terms change and vary by agency contract, always check your current FirstNet plan's documentation or your agency's account rep for the exact rules that apply to your line, rather than relying on this summary. FirstNet publishes its rate plan and data-allowance white papers directly at firstnet.com, that's the authoritative source.

Why hotspot traffic can still slow down on FirstNet

FirstNet's priority and preemption benefits apply to FirstNet-branded spectrum and infrastructure, but coverage in some areas still relies on AT&T's broader commercial network. On the shared commercial network, general network congestion and traffic management can still affect any connection, including a tethered one, the same way it can for any carrier. FirstNet doesn't publish hotspot-specific deprioritization thresholds the way consumer plans do, so there isn't a documented separate "hotspot slowdown point" to point to here.
