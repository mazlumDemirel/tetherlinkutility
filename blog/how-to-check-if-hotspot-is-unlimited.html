How to Check If Your Hotspot Is Really Unlimited

Published September 25, 2026 · Updated September 25, 2026

"Unlimited" on a phone plan usually describes the data you use on the phone itself. Hotspot data, the data your laptop or tablet uses through your phone, is very often counted separately, with its own smaller allowance. The checks below take a few minutes and tell you which kind of plan you have.

1. Look for a separate hotspot number in your plan

Open your plan's details on the carrier's site or app and look for a hotspot-specific line. Wording to watch for includes "Mobile Hotspot data", "high-speed hotspot data", "premium hotspot", "paylaşım kotası" in Turkey, or a daily quota and FUP (Fair Usage Policy) in India. If the plan lists a GB figure for hotspot, your hotspot is not unlimited at full speed, even if the plan's name says "Unlimited".

2. Check your carrier's app

Where each carrier we cover shows your usage, and what reaching the limit typically looks like. All checked September 2026 against the carrier's own support pages unless noted. Menus and plans change, so treat these as starting points and confirm in your carrier's app.

3. Check your phone's own hotspot counter

Your phone keeps its own count, which is handy for seeing how close you are before the carrier's app updates.

  • Android (Samsung wording): Settings, Connections, Data usage, Mobile data usage, then Mobile hotspot and tethering. Other Android phones have a similar entry, but menu names vary by manufacturer.
  • iPhone: Settings, Cellular, System Services, then Personal Hotspot. This counter runs since the last time statistics were reset, not per billing cycle, so it won't match your carrier's number exactly.

Signs you've reached a hotspot limit

  • The phone is fast but the laptop is slow, at the same place and the same moment. Run a speed test on both. A big gap points to a hotspot-specific limit rather than weak signal.
  • Speed sits flat at a low, round number. A hard limit looks like a steady ceiling, for example around 0.6 Mbps (600 Kbps) on Verizon or T-Mobile, or 0.128 Mbps on AT&T. Congestion looks different: speeds jump around and recover at quieter hours.
  • It started mid-cycle and returns to normal on your billing date, or at the daily reset on Indian plans with daily quotas.
  • Hotspot won't turn on, or devices connect with no internet. That usually means the plan or line has no hotspot entitlement at all, not a speed limit.

If your hotspot isn't really unlimited

  1. Buy a hotspot add-on or move to a higher plan if your carrier sells one. This is the fully supported route.
  2. Wait for the reset on your billing date, or the next day on daily-quota plans.
  3. Move large downloads to Wi-Fi and pause laptop backups and updates while tethering.
