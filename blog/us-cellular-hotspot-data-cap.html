US Cellular Hotspot Data Cap Explained

Published September 23, 2026

US Cellular's unlimited plans work the same way most major US carriers' plans do: the data you use directly on your phone is unlimited, but mobile hotspot data is tracked as its own separate, smaller bucket, and it runs out well before your on-device data ever would.

How US Cellular's hotspot allotment works

US Cellular's unlimited tiers commonly include a set amount of high-speed mobile hotspot data bundled into the plan, historically in the range of a few GB up to around 10GB depending on the specific plan and pricing tier. Once that bundled hotspot allotment is used up, hotspot speed is typically reduced for the rest of the billing cycle, even though your phone's own on-device data keeps working at full speed. Prepaid plans work a bit differently: hotspot use draws from the plan's overall high-speed data pool, and once that's gone the whole line, not just hotspot, drops to a much slower fallback speed. Exact allotments, pricing, and rules change between plan revisions, always check your current plan's terms on US Cellular's own site or app for the number that applies to you.

Heavy overall usage can also trigger a slowdown

Separately from the hotspot-specific allotment, US Cellular plans commonly include a general high-usage threshold, historically discussed around the 100GB per line per month mark, where total data use (on-device and hotspot combined) can trigger reduced speeds for the rest of the cycle during network congestion. This is a different mechanism from the hotspot-specific cap above, it's about overall heavy use rather than hotspot specifically.

Two separate limits can apply at once: a smaller hotspot-only allotment that slows tethered traffic specifically, and a much larger overall usage threshold that can affect the whole line. Check both in your plan's terms if your hotspot connection feels slow.
