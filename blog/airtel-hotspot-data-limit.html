Airtel Hotspot Data Limit Explained

Published September 21, 2026

Airtel's "Unlimited 5G" plans sound exactly like what they say on the label, until you try to share that data with a laptop or another device over hotspot. Airtel's own terms state that data sharing via mobile hotspot isn't allowed on the Unlimited 5G benefit, and the "unlimited" pool itself is capped at 300GB a month under a Fair Usage Policy. Neither of those facts is obvious from the marketing.

How Airtel's hotspot restriction works

When you turn on your phone's hotspot to connect a laptop or tablet, that secondary device's data doesn't draw from your Unlimited 5G allowance at all. Instead it falls back to your regular daily 4G data quota, often as little as 1.5GB to 2GB a day, which gets exhausted fast under any real tethering load. Airtel introduced the hotspot-sharing restriction on its Unlimited 5G plans back in 2023, and it resurfaced as a widely reported controversy in mid-2026 when users noticed hotspot usage wasn't drawing from their 5G data the way they expected. As of this writing Airtel hasn't issued a clear public clarification on the policy.

The 300GB FUP cap

Separately from the hotspot restriction, Airtel applies a 300GB Fair Usage Policy limit per 30-day billing cycle to the Unlimited 5G benefit itself. Cross 300GB of on-device 5G usage in a cycle and speeds get reduced for the rest of that cycle, "unlimited" or not.

How this compares to Jio

Jio's 5G plans (2GB/day and up) generally do permit hotspot sharing as part of the same daily allowance, no separate hotspot block. See our Jio hotspot breakdown for the specifics and its own caveats (daily, not monthly, caps).

Both restrictions (no hotspot sharing on the Unlimited 5G pool, and the 300GB FUP) are enforced by detecting that traffic is tethered in the first place, commonly via TTL decrement and traffic-pattern analysis. See our full explanation of how carrier hotspot detection works.

Your options

  1. Stay under 300GB if you're only tethering occasionally, the FUP cap is the more forgiving of the two limits.
