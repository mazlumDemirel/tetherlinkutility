US Cellular Hotspot Data Limits Explained (2026)

Published September 23, 2026 · Updated September 25, 2026

US Cellular's unlimited plans follow a pattern common among US carriers: data used directly on the phone is unlimited, while mobile hotspot data is tracked as its own, smaller allowance.

US Cellular is now part of T-Mobile

T-Mobile completed its purchase of UScellular's wireless operations (customers, stores and part of its spectrum) in August 2025. UScellular no longer signs up new customers on its own plans; new sign-ups go to T-Mobile. Existing UScellular customers were told they could keep their current plans for now or move to a T-Mobile plan without switching costs. In practice, the hotspot rules that apply to you depend on which plan you are on today: a legacy UScellular plan or a T-Mobile plan. (Checked September 2026. Plan transitions are ongoing and can change.)

What the hotspot limit is

US Cellular's unlimited tiers generally include a set amount of high-speed hotspot data, and the amount varies by plan. Once that allowance is used, hotspot speed is reduced for the rest of the billing cycle, while on-device data keeps working as before. On prepaid plans, hotspot use generally draws from the plan's overall high-speed data, and once that is used, the whole line moves to a slower speed. Exact allowances and rules differ by plan, so the terms shown in your UScellular or T-Mobile account are the ones that apply to you. If you moved to a T-Mobile plan, see our T-Mobile hotspot guide.

If hotspot does not turn on at all

On plans or lines that do not include hotspot, the phone's hotspot feature may not turn on, or connected devices may not get data. This is usually a plan setting rather than a technical fault. Contact customer support to confirm whether hotspot is included on your line and how to add it.

How to check your hotspot data

  1. Your carrier account: sign in to your UScellular account (or the T-Life app if you moved to T-Mobile) and open the usage details for your line.
  2. Your phone's data counter: Android's hotspot or data usage screen, or Settings → Cellular on iPhone, shows how much connected devices used.
  3. Customer support: ask which hotspot allowance applies to your legacy plan, since plan names and terms have changed during the transition.

For other carriers, see how to check if your hotspot is really unlimited.

Official options

  1. Check whether your plan offers a hotspot add-on, or whether a T-Mobile plan with a larger hotspot allowance suits you better.
  2. Wait for your billing cycle to reset, and use Wi-Fi for large downloads in the meantime.
  3. Ask support to confirm the current hotspot terms before changing plans.

Sharing mobile data between your own devices

If you want to share your phone's mobile data with a laptop or another phone, Tether Link Utility is an Android app that does this through a local SOCKS5 proxy. Data used by connected devices still counts toward your mobile plan, so check your carrier's tethering terms. For a comparison of the general methods (Wi-Fi hotspot, USB, Bluetooth and SOCKS5 proxy), see 5 ways to share your phone's internet with a laptop.
